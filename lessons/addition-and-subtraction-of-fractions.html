Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Driving Question: How can we combine fractions when their pieces are different sizes?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — fractions, decimals and percentages as connected representations of number.

Seeds of Change: add and subtract fractions with the same and different denominators; use equivalent fractions to create common denominators; add and subtract mixed numbers; exchange one whole when necessary; simplify final answers; and solve multi-step fraction problems.

🧠 The Knowing World

When can fractions be combined?

Fractions can be added or subtracted directly when their denominators are the same because the pieces are the same size.

3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8

When denominators are different, create equivalent fractions with a common denominator before combining the numerators.

1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4

Mixed numbers

Mixed numbers contain a whole-number part and a fractional part. Whole numbers and fractional parts can be combined separately when the fractional pieces have the same denominator.

2 1/3 + 1 1/6
1/3 = 2/6, so 2 2/6 + 1 1/6 = 3 3/6 = 3 1/2.

When subtracting, one whole may need to be exchanged for fractional parts.

3 1/4 − 1 3/4
Exchange one whole: 3 1/4 = 2 5/4.
2 5/4 − 1 3/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2.

Key vocabulary

common denominator, equivalent fraction, numerator, denominator, mixed number, improper fraction, exchange, simplify

🌍 The Human Story

Community Kitchen Recipe Planner

Community kitchens often combine ingredients from several recipes, calculate how much remains and adjust quantities for larger groups. Accurate fraction calculations help prevent shortages and reduce waste.

One recipe uses 3/4 kg of flour and another uses 2/3 kg. The pieces are measured in different fractional units, so a common denominator is needed before finding the total.

🌱 The Living Body

Pause before calculating

Fraction calculations often contain several small decisions. Work one step at a time.

  1. Check whether the denominators match.
  2. Choose a common denominator if needed.
  3. Check whether a whole must be exchanged.
  4. Calculate carefully.
  5. Simplify and estimate whether the result is sensible.

Confidence check

  • “I can add and subtract fractions with matching denominators.”
  • “I can create equivalent fractions before calculating.”
  • “I can exchange one whole and work with mixed numbers.”

Helpful self-talk

“I can slow the calculation down. I only need to make one correct decision at a time.”
