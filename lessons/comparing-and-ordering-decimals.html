Comparing and Ordering Decimals

Driving Question: Why can a number with more digits still be smaller?

🧠 The Knowing World

How do we compare decimals?

Compare decimals from left to right. Begin with the whole-number part, then compare tenths, hundredths and thousandths until the first difference appears.

To compare 4.506 and 4.56, rewrite 4.56 as 4.560.
The whole-number parts match. The tenths match. At the hundredths column, 0 hundredths is less than 6 hundredths, so 4.506 < 4.560.

Placeholder zeros

Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value.

0.8 = 0.80 = 0.800

Placeholder zeros can make comparison easier because they align corresponding place-value columns.

Inequality symbols

  • < means less than.
  • > means greater than.
  • = means equal in value.

Key vocabulary

decimal, magnitude, compare, order, inequality, equivalent, interval, tenths, hundredths, thousandths, ascending, descending

🌍 The Human Story

Decimals, prices and fair choices

Prices, weights and quantities are often written as decimals. Misreading a decimal can lead to unfair comparisons or poor financial decisions.

A product costing £3.05 is cheaper than one costing £3.50, even though the first price ends in a larger digit.

Questions about fairness

  • How might confusing £4.08 with £4.80 affect a customer?
  • Why should quantities be compared using the same units?
  • How can clear decimal notation support informed decisions?

🎨 The Expressive Self

Explain the first difference

Choose two decimals and explain exactly where their values first differ.

Sentence structure:
“The whole-number parts are equal. The tenths are equal. At the hundredths column, ___ is greater than ___, so…”

Choose how to express your thinking

  • Create an annotated place-value chart.
  • Draw a number-line explanation.
  • Record a spoken comparison.
  • Create a short teaching poster.
  • Write a dialogue between two decimals arguing about which is greater.

Try comparing: 3.9 and 3.15, 0.52 and 0.506, or 5.009 and 5.09.

🌱 The Living Body

Slow the comparison down

Similar-looking decimals can feel confusing. Compare one place-value column at a time.

  1. Align the decimal points.
  2. Add placeholder zeros if helpful.
  3. Compare whole-number parts.
  4. Compare tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths.
  5. Stop at the first difference.

Confidence check

  • “I can place decimals on a number line.”
  • “I can compare decimals using corresponding columns.”
  • “I can order several decimals and justify my sequence.”

Helpful self-talk

“More digits do not automatically mean a greater value. I can compare corresponding places.”

Assignment: Design a Fair Decimal Comparison Guide

Create a poster, slide, report or digital guide that teaches another learner how to compare and order decimals accurately.

Your final product must include:

  • at least six decimals ordered from smallest to largest
  • a number line showing at least four of the decimals
  • three correct inequality statements
  • one pair of equivalent decimals written differently
  • one comparison decided by the tenths column
  • one comparison decided by the hundredths column

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Mastering
Conceptual Understanding I can compare some familiar decimals using a model or prompt. I can explain comparisons using tenths and hundredths in familiar examples. Fluency & Accuracy I can order a small set of decimals with support. I can compare and order familiar decimals, though I may make occasional placeholder errors. Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar price or measure comparison when a model is provided. I can compare familiar prices and measures with some support. I can apply decimal comparison to money, mass, capacity and fair-choice problems. I can design, evaluate and refine decimal comparison problems involving subtle constraints.

I can choose a reliable strategy, check my ordering and explain what helped me. I can work independently, compare strategies and justify which representation is most effective.
