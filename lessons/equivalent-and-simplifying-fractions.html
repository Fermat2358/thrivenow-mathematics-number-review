Equivalent Fractions and Simplifying Fractions

Driving Question: How can a fraction change its appearance without changing its value?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — fractions, decimals and percentages as connected representations of parts and wholes.

Seeds of Change: identify and generate equivalent fractions; use common factors to simplify fractions; explain why multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number preserves value; and use simplest form when comparing and solving problems.

🧠 The Knowing World

Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions have different numerators and denominators but represent the same amount.

1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8

When the numerator and denominator are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number, the value of the fraction stays the same.

Simplifying fractions

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by a common factor. A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.

Example: 18/24 → divide both by 6 → 3/4

Key vocabulary

numerator, denominator, equivalent, common factor, simplest form, fraction bar, whole, equal parts

🌍 The Human Story

Fair Shares at the Community Café

Sharing food fairly often involves equivalent fractions. Two people may receive pieces cut into different shapes or numbers of parts while still receiving the same amount.

One tray is cut into 6 equal pieces and a learner receives 2 pieces. Another tray is cut into 3 equal pieces and a learner receives 1 piece. Both shares are equal because 2/6 = 1/3.

Fairness inquiry

  • Does fair always mean the same number of pieces?
  • What matters more: the number of pieces or the total amount?
  • How can simplifying a fraction make a fair share easier to understand?

🎨 The Expressive Self

Show equivalence in your own way

Choose one equivalent fraction family and communicate why all the fractions represent the same amount.

Choose from: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6   |   1/3 = 2/6 = 3/9   |   2/5 = 4/10 = 6/15

Choose a form

  • Draw a fraction bar or fraction wall.
  • Create a comic where fractions discover they belong to the same family.
  • Write a short explanation using numerator, denominator and common factor.
  • Record a spoken explanation.
  • Invent a metaphor for simplifying, such as “removing matching layers”.

Sentence stem: “These fractions are equivalent because…”

🌱 The Living Body

Pause before simplifying

A fraction can look complicated even when its value is familiar. Pause before calculating and look for a common factor.

  1. Look at the numerator and denominator.
  2. Ask: what number divides both exactly?
  3. Divide both by the same factor.
  4. Check whether another common factor remains.

Confidence check

  • “I can recognise some equivalent fractions visually.”
  • “I can simplify when I know a common factor.”
  • “I can explain why the fraction’s value stays the same.”

Helpful self-talk

“I do not need to find the greatest common factor immediately. I can simplify in more than one step.”

🛠 Final Task

Assignment: Create an Equivalent Fraction Gallery

Design a poster, slide, report or digital display that teaches another learner how equivalent fractions and simplifying work.

Your final product must include:

  • at least three equivalent fraction families
  • a visual fraction bar or fraction wall
  • two fractions simplified using common factors
  • one example simplified in more than one step
  • an explanation of why dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number preserves value
  • one fair-sharing example
  • a short reflection explaining which strategy helped most

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to use equivalence flexibly, simplify fractions efficiently, compare rational numbers and justify multiplicative relationships.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Extending
Conceptual Understanding I can recognise some fractions that look the same in a visual model. I can identify equivalent fractions using bars, walls or prompts. I can explain why equivalent fractions have the same value and why simplifying preserves value. I can generalise how multiplying or dividing both parts by the same factor creates equivalent fractions.
Fluency & Accuracy I can simplify some familiar fractions with support. I can use common factors to simplify fractions, though I may stop before simplest form. I can generate equivalent fractions and simplify accurately to simplest form. I can select efficient common factors and check my results using another method.
Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar fraction problem when the model is provided. I can use equivalence in familiar sharing and comparison problems with some support. I can apply equivalent fractions and simplifying to explain and solve practical problems. I can design and justify my own visual or real-life fraction model.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and identify when I need help. I can use a suggested model or factor strategy and respond to feedback. I can choose a strategy, check my work and explain what helped me learn. I can work independently, refine my explanation and compare different simplifying methods.
