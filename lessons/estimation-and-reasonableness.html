Estimation and Reasonableness

🧠 The Knowing World

What makes an estimate useful?

An estimate is a deliberately simplified calculation that gives an approximate answer. It should be quick enough to use mentally but close enough to reveal the expected size of the exact answer.

48.7 × 19.6
48.7 is approximately 50 and 19.6 is approximately 20.
50 × 20 = 1,000.
Therefore, an exact answer near 1,000 is reasonable.

Useful estimation strategies

  • Round to one significant figure.
  • Round to nearby multiples of 10, 100 or 1,000.
  • Use compatible numbers that are easy to calculate with.
  • Round up when checking whether a budget is definitely sufficient.
  • Use an inverse operation to check the approximate result.

Reasonableness checks

  1. Should the answer be larger or smaller than the starting values?
  2. What approximate size should the answer have?
  3. Could the decimal point be misplaced?
  4. Does the proposed answer lie near the estimate?
  5. Can an inverse operation support the result?

Key vocabulary

estimate, approximate, exact, reasonable, magnitude, compatible numbers, inverse operation, overestimate, underestimate, quotient, product

🌍 The Human Story

Estimation in community planning

Event organisers often need quick estimates before exact figures are known. Estimates can support budgeting, transport, seating, food orders and safe capacity planning.

About 198 people are attending an event. Tables seat 8 people each.
200 ÷ 8 = 25, so approximately 25 tables are needed.

Fairness and safety

  • When should an organiser deliberately overestimate?
  • Who might be excluded if seating or food is underestimated?
  • Why should an estimate be updated when exact information becomes available?

Route requirements

  • A checkpoint about whether the answer should increase or decrease.
  • A checkpoint that creates an estimate.
  • A checkpoint about expected magnitude.
  • A checkpoint for possible decimal-point errors.
  • A checkpoint using an inverse operation.
  • A final decision: reasonable, revise or recalculate.
  • At least one test using an unreasonable answer.
🌱 The Living Body

Pause before accepting an answer

Calculator answers can look convincing even when a key has been pressed incorrectly. A short pause can protect against accepting an unreasonable result.

  1. Notice the operation.
  2. Predict whether the answer should be larger or smaller.
  3. Create a simple estimate.
  4. Compare the exact result with the estimate.
  5. Use an inverse operation if uncertainty remains.

Confidence check

  • “I can estimate before calculating.”
  • “I can identify an answer that is too large or too small.”
  • “I can use an inverse operation to check.”
  • “I can explain why an estimate is useful for a particular decision.”

Helpful self-talk

“I do not have to calculate exactly to know whether an answer makes sense.”

This task prepares learners to select efficient approximations, assess numerical magnitude, check calculations independently and apply estimation to multi-step practical problems.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Mastering
Conceptual Understanding I can recognise that an estimate is an approximate answer when given support. I can explain how rounding creates an estimate in familiar calculations. I can explain magnitude, overestimation, underestimation and how estimates test reasonableness. I can compare estimation strategies and generalise when each is most useful.
Fluency & Accuracy I can estimate some simple sums or products using suggested rounded values. I can estimate familiar calculations, though my approximations may sometimes be inefficient. I can efficiently estimate all four operations with integers and decimals. I can select and compare several useful approximations and verify their accuracy independently.
Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar estimation problem when the method is provided. I can use estimation in familiar money, seating or quantity problems with some support. I can apply estimation to budgets, capacity and safety decisions and justify rounding choices. I can design, evaluate and refine estimation plans in unfamiliar multi-step contexts.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and identify when an answer may need checking. I can use a suggested estimate and respond to feedback. I can choose an approximation, use an inverse check and explain what helped me. I can independently build and refine a checking system and justify the most effective strategy.
