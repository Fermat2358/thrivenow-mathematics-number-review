Adding and Subtracting Negative Integers

Driving Question: How can subtracting make a number larger?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — Number Sense & Operations — place value, estimation and integers.

Seeds of Change: add and subtract positive and negative integers; interpret calculations as movement and change; use zero pairs and additive inverses; understand subtraction as adding the opposite; calculate across zero; and check results using inverse operations and representations.

National Curriculum learning: use the four operations with positive and negative integers; recognise and use inverse relationships; use number lines and other representations to support directed-number calculations; and solve problems involving temperature, elevation, scores and balances.

🧠 The Knowing World

Adding integers

Adding a positive integer moves a value in the positive direction. Adding a negative integer moves a value in the negative direction.

−3 + 7
Start at −3 and move 7 places to the right.
The result is 4.

4 + (−9)
Start at 4 and move 9 places to the left.
The result is −5.

Zero pairs

One positive tile and one negative tile combine to make zero. They form a zero pair.

+1 + (−1) = 0

Subtracting integers

Subtracting an integer means adding its opposite.

6 − 4 = 6 + (−4) = 2
6 − (−4) = 6 + 4 = 10

The two negative signs in 6 − (−4) have different jobs: the first is the subtraction operation and the second belongs to the integer −4.

Reliable strategy

  1. Keep the first integer unchanged.
  2. Change subtraction to addition.
  3. Replace the second integer with its opposite.
  4. Calculate the new addition.
  5. Check the direction and magnitude using a number line or inverse.

🌍 The Human Story

Temperature changes happen in sequences

Weather forecasts often describe a starting temperature followed by rises and falls. Signed integers help communicate both direction and size.

Morning: −6°C
Midday change: +9°C
Evening change: −5°C
Final temperature: −2°C

Why accuracy matters

  • Travel plans may depend on whether temperatures remain below zero.
  • Heating and shelter decisions can be affected by forecast errors.
  • A small sign error can reverse the meaning of a temperature change.

🌱 The Living Body

Pause between the signs

Expressions containing two signs can feel visually crowded. Read each sign separately before calculating.

  1. Identify the first integer.
  2. Name the operation.
  3. Identify the sign of the second integer.
  4. Transform subtraction into addition of the opposite when helpful.
  5. Choose tiles, movement or symbols.
  6. Check whether the answer’s direction makes sense.

Confidence check

  • “I can make and cancel zero pairs.”
  • “I can add integers with the same sign.”
  • “I can add integers with different signs.”
  • “I can rewrite subtraction as addition of the opposite.”

Helpful self-talk

“The two signs may have different jobs. I can read the operation and the integer separately.”

Assignment: Design an Integer Journey Experience

Your final product must include:

  • one addition of two negative integers
  • one addition involving different signs
  • one subtraction of a positive integer
  • one subtraction of a negative integer
  • one calculation represented using zero pairs
  • one calculation transformed into addition of the opposite
  • one journey that crosses zero
  • one real-world temperature or elevation context
  • one deliberate misconception and correction
  • one inverse or representation-based check

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to calculate fluently with directed numbers, select representations strategically, interpret multiple signs correctly and apply integer operations in multi-step contexts.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Mastering
Conceptual Understanding I can recognise positive and negative integers and follow a model with support. I can explain simple integer addition using movement or tiles. I can explain zero pairs, additive inverses and subtraction as adding the opposite. I can connect tile, number-line and symbolic models and justify why they are equivalent.
Fluency & Accuracy I can complete some simple integer calculations using prompts. I can add integers accurately but may confuse some subtraction signs. I can accurately add and subtract positive and negative integers, including across zero. I can calculate efficiently with unfamiliar multi-step expressions and verify results independently.
Application to Problems I can interpret a simple temperature rise or fall with support. I can solve familiar one-step temperature, score or elevation problems. I can solve multi-step directed-number problems and explain the meaning of each sign. I can design, evaluate and debug integer journeys in unfamiliar contexts.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and identify when I need a model. I can use a suggested representation and respond to feedback. I can choose a representation, check my result and explain what helped me. I can independently compare representations, debug errors and justify the most effective method.
