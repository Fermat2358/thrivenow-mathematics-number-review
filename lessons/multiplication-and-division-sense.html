🧠 The Knowing World

What does multiplication do?

Multiplication can describe repeated addition, equal groups, scaling or the area of a rectangle. Its effect depends on the multiplier.

  • Multiplying by a number greater than 1 makes a positive number larger.
  • Multiplying by 1 keeps the number unchanged.
  • Multiplying by a positive number between 0 and 1 makes the number smaller.

Examples:
8 × 3 = 24, so the result is greater than 8.
8 × 1 = 8, so the value is unchanged.
8 × 0.5 = 4, so the result is smaller than 8.

What does division do?

Division can describe sharing, grouping or inverse scaling. Its effect depends on the divisor.

  • Dividing by a number greater than 1 makes a positive number smaller.
  • Dividing by 1 keeps the number unchanged.
  • Dividing by a positive number between 0 and 1 makes the number larger.

Examples:
8 ÷ 4 = 2, so the result is smaller than 8.
8 ÷ 1 = 8, so the value is unchanged.
8 ÷ 0.5 = 16, because there are sixteen halves in 8.

The distributive law

A factor can be partitioned into useful parts without changing the product.

23 × 16
= 23 × (10 + 6)
= 23 × 10 + 23 × 6
= 230 + 138
= 368

🌍 The Human Story

Scaling resources fairly

Community organisations often need to scale recipes, seating, transport and equipment. A multiplication or division error can lead to waste, shortage or exclusion.

A meal for 8 people needs 1.6 kg of rice. To serve 40 people, the recipe must be scaled by a factor of 5: 1.6 × 5 = 8 kg.

Questions about fairness and responsibility

  • Who might be affected if a resource calculation is too small?
  • When might a small overestimate be safer than an underestimate?
  • How can an inverse check prevent waste?

🌱 The Living Body

Predict before calculating

Multiplication and division symbols can trigger automatic rules. Pause and inspect the multiplier or divisor before deciding what the answer should look like.

  1. Notice whether the operation is multiplication or division.
  2. Compare the multiplier or divisor with 1.
  3. Predict whether the answer should increase, decrease or stay equal.
  4. Estimate the likely magnitude.
  5. Calculate and check with the inverse operation.

Confidence check

  • “I can predict the effect of multiplying by a decimal.”
  • “I can predict the effect of dividing by a decimal.”
  • “I can use an area model to partition a product.”
  • “I can check multiplication and division using inverse operations.”

Helpful self-talk

“I can compare the multiplier or divisor with 1 before I calculate.”

Reflection

  • A multiplication result that became smaller was...
  • A division result that became larger was...
  • An area-model partition that helped me was...

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Mastering
Conceptual Understanding I can identify multiplication and division and use familiar models with support. I can explain multiplication as equal groups and division as sharing or grouping. I can explain scaling, inverse relationships, the distributive law and the effect of values above or below 1. I can generalise operation effects and connect equal groups, area, scaling and inverse representations.
Fluency & Accuracy I can complete some familiar multiplication or division calculations with support. I can use known facts and simple partitioning, though I may make occasional place-value errors. I can accurately multiply and divide integers and decimals using suitable strategies. I can work efficiently with unfamiliar values, compare methods and verify products and quotients independently.
Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar equal-groups or sharing problem when the operation is identified. I can solve familiar scaling and resource problems with some support. I can select and apply multiplication or division in multi-step resource and measure problems. I can design, evaluate and refine scaling plans involving unfamiliar constraints and decimal quantities.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and recognise when an answer may need checking. I can use a suggested model or strategy and respond to feedback. I can predict the operation’s effect, estimate, calculate and check using the inverse. I can independently compare representations, refine my method and justify the most effective strategy.
