KS3 Foundations in Number

Understanding Negative Integers

Driving Question: How can a number be less than nothing?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — Number Sense & Operations — place value, estimation and integers.

Seeds of Change: recognise negative integers in mathematical and real-world contexts; locate and order signed integers; compare values using <, > and =; understand zero as a reference point; identify opposites and additive inverses; and interpret changes that cross zero.

National Curriculum learning: order positive and negative integers; use horizontal and vertical number lines; understand directed number; and solve problems involving temperature, height, depth, money and change.

🧠 The Knowing World

Negative integers describe values below a reference point

Negative integers appear to the left of zero on a horizontal number line and below zero on a vertical number line.

−6 < −2 < 0 < 4

The farther left a number lies, the smaller its value. This means −8 is smaller than −3, even though 8 is a larger digit than 3.

Zero is the reference point

  • Positive integers are greater than zero.
  • Negative integers are less than zero.
  • Zero is neither positive nor negative.

Opposites and additive inverses

Two integers are opposites when they are the same distance from zero but lie on opposite sides.

5 and −5 are opposites.
5 + (−5) = 0

The opposite of an integer is also called its additive inverse. Zero is its own opposite.

🌍 The Human Story

Negative temperatures and community decisions

Temperatures below zero are represented using negative integers. Correctly comparing these values matters when planning travel, heating, clothing and community support.

−8°C is colder than −3°C because −8 lies farther below zero.

Changes across zero

A change from 4°C to −3°C crosses zero. The total fall is 7°C: four degrees to reach zero and three more degrees below zero.

🌱 The Living Body

Pause and picture the number line

Negative signs can be easy to overlook. Slow down and imagine the positions before comparing the digits.

  1. Locate zero.
  2. Decide which side of zero each value lies on.
  3. Picture the values on a number line.
  4. Remember that farther right means greater.
  5. Check whether the question asks for value or distance from zero.

Confidence check

  • “I can place negative integers on a number line.”
  • “I can compare two negative integers.”
  • “I can identify an integer and its opposite.”
  • “I can describe a temperature change that crosses zero.”

Helpful self-talk

“I compare positions, not just digits. The value farther to the right is greater.”

🛠 Final Task

Assignment: Design a Below-Zero Community Information Guide

Create a poster, report, slide or digital guide that teaches another learner how to interpret and compare negative integers.

Your final product must include:

  • a horizontal or vertical number line containing at least eight integers
  • at least three negative integers, zero and three positive integers
  • an ordered list from least to greatest
  • three correct inequality statements
  • two pairs of opposite integers
  • an explanation of additive inverses
  • a temperature comparison
  • a temperature change that crosses zero
  • one corrected misconception
  • an integer-comparison decision rule or flowchart
  • a reflection explaining why −8 is smaller than −3

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to compare directed numbers, reason about position and magnitude, understand additive inverses and interpret signed values in unfamiliar contexts.
