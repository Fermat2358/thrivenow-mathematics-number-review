KS3 Foundations in Number

Decimal Place Value and Scaling

Driving Question: How can a digit become ten, one hundred or one thousand times greater without changing its identity?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — decimals within the wider fractions, decimals and percentages sequence.

Seeds of Change: identify the value of digits in numbers with up to three decimal places; read, write, compare and order decimals; understand tenths, hundredths and thousandths; multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1,000; explain how digit values change across place-value columns; and solve practical problems involving decimal measures.

🧠 The Knowing World

Decimal place value

The decimal point separates whole-number places from fractional places. Each place is worth ten times as much as the place immediately to its right.

Thousands Hundreds Tens Ones . Tenths Hundredths Thousandths
0 0 1 2 . 4 0 8

In 12.408, the 4 represents four tenths, the 0 is a placeholder in the hundredths column, and the 8 represents eight thousandths.

Scaling by powers of ten

  • Multiplying by 10 makes every digit ten times as valuable.
  • Multiplying by 100 makes every digit one hundred times as valuable.
  • Multiplying by 1,000 makes every digit one thousand times as valuable.
  • Dividing reverses these changes.

Important: the decimal point does not travel through the number. The digits change columns in a fixed place-value system.

4.206 × 100 = 420.6

🌍 The Human Story

Decimals in money and measurement

Decimal place value helps people measure accurately, compare prices and scale quantities. Small place-value errors can have large effects when quantities are multiplied for many people.

If one fruit bag contains 0.375 kg, ten bags contain 3.75 kg, not 0.3750 kg.

Fairness and accuracy

  • Why should market scales show decimals accurately?
  • How could a misplaced digit affect a customer?
  • Why are unit conversions important when comparing products?

🌱 The Living Body

Pause before scaling

Decimal calculations can feel uncertain when zeros appear. Use the place-value structure rather than trying to remember a shortcut.

  1. Name the operation.
  2. Predict whether the answer should be larger or smaller.
  3. Count how many place-value columns each digit changes.
  4. Use placeholder zeros where needed.
  5. Reverse the operation to check.

Confidence check

  • “I can identify tenths, hundredths and thousandths.”
  • “I can explain how digits change value when scaling.”
  • “I can multiply and divide decimals by 10, 100 and 1,000.”

Helpful self-talk

“The decimal point is fixed in the chart. I can track each digit one column at a time.”
