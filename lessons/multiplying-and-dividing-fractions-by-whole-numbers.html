Multiplying Fractions and Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers

Driving Question: What happens to a fraction when we repeat it or share it equally?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — fractions, decimals and percentages as connected representations of number.

Seeds of Change: multiply proper fractions and mixed numbers by whole numbers; understand multiplication as repeated addition and scaling; divide proper fractions by whole numbers through equal sharing; simplify answers; convert improper fractions to mixed numbers; and solve practical fraction problems.

🧠 The Knowing World

Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

Multiplying a fraction by a whole number can be understood as repeated addition.

3 × 2/5 = 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5

The denominator stays the same because the size of each fractional piece does not change. The numerator is multiplied because the number of pieces increases.

Dividing a fraction by a whole number

Dividing a fraction by a whole number means sharing the fractional amount equally.

3/4 ÷ 3 = 1/4

Three quarters shared equally between three groups gives one quarter to each group. When the numerator does not divide exactly, the fraction can be rewritten in smaller equivalent parts.

Example: 2/3 ÷ 4 = 2/12 = 1/6.

Reasonableness checks

  • Multiplying by a whole number greater than 1 should make the quantity larger.
  • Dividing by a whole number greater than 1 should make each share smaller.
  • An improper fraction can be rewritten as a mixed number.
  • Final answers should be simplified where possible.

Key vocabulary

multiply, divide, repeated addition, equal sharing, proper fraction, improper fraction, mixed number, simplify, scale

🌍 The Human Story

Scaling food for a community meal

Community kitchens often multiply ingredient quantities when preparing several batches of food. Leftover ingredients may then be divided equally between cooking stations, families or storage containers.

If each tray needs 3/4 kg of vegetables, then five trays need 5 × 3/4 kg.

Fairness and sustainability

  • Why is accurate scaling important when many people depend on a meal?
  • How can equal sharing prevent waste or shortage?
  • What might happen if a fractional quantity is multiplied or divided incorrectly?

🌱 The Living Body

Predict before calculating

Before multiplying or dividing, pause and predict how the size of the quantity should change.

  1. Name the starting fraction.
  2. Notice whether you are multiplying or dividing.
  3. Predict whether the answer should be larger or smaller.
  4. Calculate one step at a time.
  5. Simplify and compare the answer with your prediction.

Confidence check

  • “I can model repeated addition using fraction bars.”
  • “I can divide a fractional amount into equal shares.”
  • “I can calculate, simplify and check whether my answer is sensible.”

Helpful self-talk

“I can picture what is happening before I use a rule. My estimate can help me notice an error.”

Reflection

  • An equal-sharing model that helped me was...
  • A prediction that helped me check an answer was...

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to calculate with rational numbers, interpret multiplication as scaling, understand division as equal sharing and justify whether a result is reasonable.
