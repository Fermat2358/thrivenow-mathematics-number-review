🧠 The Knowing World

Separate sign from magnitude

A product or quotient has two parts: its sign and its magnitude.

−7 × 6
Different signs give a negative result.
7 × 6 = 42.
Therefore, −7 × 6 = −42.

−48 ÷ −6
The signs are the same, so the quotient is positive.
48 ÷ 6 = 8.
Therefore, −48 ÷ −6 = 8.

Sign patterns

First sign Second sign Result
Positive Positive Positive
Positive Negative Negative
Negative Positive Negative
Negative Negative Positive

Why does negative × negative become positive?

Consider the sequence:

−3 × 3 = −9
−3 × 2 = −6
−3 × 1 = −3
−3 × 0 = 0
−3 × (−1) = 3
−3 × (−2) = 6

Each time the second factor decreases by 1, the product increases by 3. The pattern must continue consistently through zero.

Inverse relationships

If −6 × 7 = −42, then:
−42 ÷ 7 = −6
−42 ÷ −6 = 7

🌍 The Human Story

Repeated rises and descents

Signed multiplication can describe repeated changes. A positive result may represent a rise, while a negative result may represent a descent.

Four stages each involve a descent of 35 metres:
4 × (−35) = −140 metres.

Division can describe how a total signed change is distributed equally.

A total change of −180 metres occurs over six equal stages:
−180 ÷ 6 = −30 metres per stage.

🌱 The Living Body

Two decisions, not one

When a calculation includes negative signs, it can help to separate the task into two manageable questions.

  1. What sign should the answer have?
  2. What is the magnitude of the answer?
  3. Combine the two parts.
  4. Use the inverse operation to check.

Confidence check

  • “I can decide the sign before calculating.”
  • “I can multiply integers with different signs.”
  • “I can divide integers with the same signs.”
  • “I can use an inverse fact to check.”

Helpful self-talk

“First the sign, then the size, then the check.”
