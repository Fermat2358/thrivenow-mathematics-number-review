Number and Place Value

Driving Question: Can one digit change the whole story of a number?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — Who am I as a mathematician? Number sense, place value and the four operations.
Seed of Change: Read, write, order and compare numbers up to 10,000,000; determine the value of each digit; round whole numbers; use negative numbers in context; and solve number and practical problems involving place value and rounding.

🧠 The Knowing World

Learning focus

Today we are exploring how digits behave differently depending on their place. The digit 7 has a different value in 7,000,000, 70,000 and 507.

Key knowledge

  • A number is built from digits.
  • Each digit has a place and a value.
  • In our base-10 number system, each place is worth 10 times the place to its right.
  • We can compare numbers by looking from the largest place value first.
  • Rounding helps us estimate and check whether an answer is reasonable.

Core vocabulary

digit, place value, million, hundred thousand, ten thousand, thousand, integer, negative, compare, order, round, estimate, approximately

Teacher modelling

Write the number 3,704,582.

  • What is the value of the digit 7?
  • What is the value of the digit 4?
  • How do you know?

Check for understanding: In 5,608,391, what is the value of the 6? What is the value of the 8?

🌍 The Human Story

Why does place value matter in real life?

Large numbers appear in news stories, sport, science, money, population, distances and the environment. A single digit in the wrong place can change a number by thousands, millions, or more.

Inquiry prompt

Imagine a news headline says:

“A city has spent £4,500,000 on a new community project.”

Discuss:

  • How would the meaning change if the number was £450,000?
  • How would it change if the number was £45,000,000?
  • Why do commas, zeros and digit positions matter?

Fairness and accuracy

When people talk about money, population or resources, numbers can shape decisions. Understanding place value helps us ask: Is this number accurate? Is it being used fairly? What is the real scale?

🎨 The Expressive Self

My Number Story

Choose a 7-digit number. It could be invented, connected to a place, a population, a distance, money, or a personal interest.

Write or draw your number story using at least three of these sentence starters:

  • My number is...
  • The digit with the greatest value is...
  • The digit with the smallest value is...
  • If I moved the digit ___ one place to the left, its value would...
  • My number rounded to the nearest thousand is...
  • My number rounded to the nearest million is...
  • A real-life situation where this number could appear is...

Choice of expression

  • Write a short explanation.
  • Create a place-value poster.
  • Make a comic strip where a digit “moves house”.
  • Explain your number to a partner using counters, cards or a place-value grid.

🛠 Final Task

Assignment: Place Value Detective Report

Create a short report, poster or slide explaining a mystery 7-digit number. Your final product must include:

  • One 7-digit number written in numerals and words.
  • The value of at least four digits in the number.
  • The number rounded to the nearest 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and 1,000,000.
  • A comparison with two other numbers using <, > or =.
  • A real-life context where your number could appear.
  • A short reflection explaining what helped you understand the number.

KS3 readiness link

This prepares learners for Key Stage 3 number work by strengthening fluency with integers, powers of 10, estimation, comparison, rounding and reasoning about the size of numbers.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Extending
Conceptual Understanding I can identify some digits in a number but may need support to explain their value. I can explain the value of some digits using a place-value grid or prompt. I can explain how digit position changes value in numbers up to 10,000,000. I can generalise how the base-10 system works and explain what happens when digits move left or right.
Fluency & Accuracy I can read or write some large numbers with support. I can read, write and order large numbers, though I may make some place-value or rounding errors. I can accurately read, write, order, compare and round numbers up to 10,000,000. I can work accurately and efficiently, checking my answers using estimation and place-value reasoning.
Application to Problems I can attempt a place-value problem when the steps are given to me. I can use place value to solve familiar problems, such as comparing or rounding numbers. I can apply place value to solve practical problems and explain my method clearly. I can design my own place-value challenge, test different possibilities and justify why my solution works.
Independence & Reflection I can try the task with support and identify when I feel stuck. I can use a strategy, such as a place-value grid, digit cards or a number line, when prompted. I can choose a helpful strategy, check my work and explain what helped me learn. I can work independently, improve my answer after feedback and reflect on how my thinking changed.
