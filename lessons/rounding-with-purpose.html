🧠 The Knowing World

What does rounding do?

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby value that is easier to communicate or calculate with. The rounded number is an approximation, not usually the exact value.

A reliable rounding process

  1. Identify the place to which you are rounding.
  2. Find the two neighbouring multiples or values.
  3. Inspect the digit immediately to the right of the rounding place.
  4. If that digit is 5 or more, round up.
  5. If that digit is below 5, keep the rounding digit unchanged.
  6. Replace later whole-number digits with zeros, or remove later decimal digits.

Example: Round 6,482 to the nearest hundred.
The neighbouring hundreds are 6,400 and 6,500.
The tens digit is 8, so 6,482 rounds to 6,500.

Decimal places

Decimal places are counted from the decimal point.

53.784 to 1 decimal place:
Keep the 7 tenths. Inspect the 8 hundredths. The answer is 53.8.

Significant figures

Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit.

0.04782 to 3 significant figures:
Count 4, 7 and 8 as the first three significant digits. Inspect the 2. The answer is 0.0478.

🌍 The Human Story

How accurate should information be?

The most useful degree of accuracy depends on the purpose. A rough attendance estimate may be useful for ordering refreshments, while money records or safety measurements may need greater precision.

A project cost of £48,672 might be reported as about £49,000 in an early public summary, but the exact value may be needed for an invoice.

Fairness and responsibility

  • Who could be affected if a quantity is rounded too much?
  • Can a rounded statement be mathematically reasonable but still misleading?
  • Why should the degree of accuracy match the decision being made?

🌱 The Living Body

Slow the instruction down

Rounding errors often happen because the instruction is read too quickly. Pause before changing any digits.

  1. Read the required accuracy twice.
  2. Point to the digit that will remain.
  3. Identify the deciding digit immediately to its right.
  4. Picture the neighbouring rounded values.
  5. Check that the answer has the correct form.

Confidence check

  • “I can identify the rounding place.”
  • “I can use neighbouring values and a midpoint.”
  • “I can distinguish decimal places from significant figures.”
  • “I can choose a sensible degree of accuracy for a context.”

Helpful self-talk

“I only need one deciding digit. I can locate the rounding place before I change anything.”
