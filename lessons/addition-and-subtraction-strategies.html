KS3 Foundations in Number

Choosing Efficient Addition and Subtraction Strategies

Driving Question: Is the longest calculation method always the safest?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — Number Sense & Operations — place value, estimation and integers.

Seeds of Change: add and subtract integers and decimals accurately; align corresponding place-value columns; use partitioning, compensation, bridging and formal written methods; interpret subtraction as removal, difference and change; select an efficient strategy; and check answers using estimation and inverse operations.

National Curriculum learning: use the four operations with integers and decimals; select and use appropriate calculation strategies; recognise and use inverse relationships; and use approximation to check the reasonableness of results.

🧠 The Knowing World

One calculation can invite several methods

An efficient strategy is one that is accurate, understandable and well matched to the structure of the numbers. The same method will not always be best for every calculation.

Calculation structure Possible efficient strategy Example
A number is close to a multiple of 10, 100 or 1,000 Compensation 398 + 247 = 400 + 245
The numbers are close together Count up to find the difference 1,004 − 998 = 6
One number can be split into useful parts Partitioning or bridging 376 + 248 = 376 + 200 + 40 + 8
Several regroupings or decimals are involved Formal column method 47.36 + 18.95

Compensation

Compensation changes a calculation into an equivalent but easier one. Any adjustment must preserve the original total or difference.

Addition: 398 + 247 = 400 + 245 = 645
Two were added to 398, so two must be removed from 247.

Subtraction: 603 − 298 = 605 − 300 = 305
Adding the same amount to both numbers preserves their difference.

Checking a result

  • Estimate the likely size of the answer.
  • Use the inverse operation.
  • Compare the result with the original values.
  • Check decimal alignment and placeholder zeros.

Key vocabulary

partition, compensate, bridge, difference, total, inverse, regroup, placeholder, efficient, equivalent calculation

🌍 The Human Story

Accurate calculations support responsible decisions

Community groups often work with limited budgets. A small addition or subtraction error can affect whether a project can pay for equipment, food, transport or access needs.

Budget: £1,250.00
Equipment: £398.75
Venue: £475.50
Refreshments: £186.90

Questions about fairness and responsibility

  • Who could be affected by an incorrect budget total?
  • Why should an estimate be used before accepting an exact answer?
  • When might a slower method be the more responsible choice?

🌱 The Living Body

Pause before choosing a method

A familiar written method can feel reassuring, but it is not always the easiest route. Take a short pause to notice the structure of the numbers first.

  1. Estimate the likely answer.
  2. Notice whether the numbers are close together.
  3. Look for a nearby multiple of 10, 100 or 1,000.
  4. Decide whether a mental or written method feels clearer.
  5. Check using the inverse operation.

Confidence check

  • “I can choose between more than one strategy.”
  • “I can explain why compensation works.”
  • “I can align decimal place-value columns.”
  • “I can use an inverse operation to check.”

Helpful self-talk

“I can choose the method that makes the numbers easier to see.”

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to calculate fluently with integers and decimals, select strategies independently, interpret subtraction flexibly and verify results using inverse operations and estimation.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Mastering
Conceptual Understanding I can identify addition and subtraction and follow a suggested method with support. I can explain familiar strategies such as partitioning or counting up. I can explain why compensation, bridging, inverse operations and place-value alignment work. I can compare strategy structures, generalise when each method is efficient and justify equivalent transformations.
Fluency & Accuracy I can complete some familiar addition and subtraction calculations with support. I can use mental or written methods, though I may make occasional regrouping or alignment errors. I can accurately add and subtract integers and decimals using an appropriate strategy. I can calculate efficiently with unfamiliar values, compare methods and verify results independently.
Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar money or difference problem when the operation is identified. I can solve familiar budget and change problems with some support. I can choose and apply efficient strategies in multi-step money, measure and comparison problems. I can design, evaluate and refine calculation plans in unfamiliar contexts with meaningful constraints.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and recognise when I need help. I can use a suggested strategy and respond to feedback. I can select a strategy, estimate, check with an inverse and explain what helped me. I can independently compare methods, refine my approach and justify the most effective strategy.
