KS3 Foundations in Number

Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

Driving Question: How can the same amount wear three different disguises?

Explore one way at a time: open a section below. Opening another Way of Knowing will close the one you were using.

Learning pathway: KS3 Foundations in Number — fractions, decimals and percentages as connected representations of number.

Seeds of Change: recognise common fraction, decimal and percentage equivalents; simplify fractions; use place value and division to convert between forms; compare equivalent quantities; and apply equivalence in practical problems.

🧠 The Knowing World

One quantity, three representations

A fraction, decimal and percentage can name exactly the same amount.

1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

Useful relationships

  • A fraction describes a number of equal parts.
  • A decimal uses place value to show tenths, hundredths and beyond.
  • A percentage means “out of 100”.
  • To change a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.
  • To change a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100.
  • To change a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100.

Common equivalents

Fraction Decimal Percentage
1/2 0.5 50%
1/4 0.25 25%
3/4 0.75 75%
1/5 0.2 20%
1/10 0.1 10%

🌍 The Human Story

Why do shops use percentages?

Fractions, decimals and percentages are mathematically equivalent, but they may not feel equally clear to every person. Shops often use percentages because they make it easier to compare discounts.

A sign saying 25% off, 0.25 off or 1/4 off describes the same proportion, but one representation may be easier for a shopper to interpret quickly.

Fairness and communication

  • Which representation is easiest to understand?
  • Could a confusing sign affect somebody’s decision?
  • Should shops show more than one representation?
  • How could mathematics make pricing more transparent?

🎨 The Expressive Self

Explain equivalence in your own way

  • Create a comic where three characters discover they are equal.
  • Write a short explanation using mathematical vocabulary.
  • Record a spoken explanation.
  • Invent a metaphor such as “three different names for the same place”.

🌱 The Living Body

Notice before converting

Converting between forms can feel like several different tasks. Pause and remind yourself that the quantity does not change—only its representation changes.

  1. Name the form you have been given.
  2. Name the form you need.
  3. Choose one conversion step.
  4. Check whether the answer has a sensible size.

Confidence check

  • “I recognise some common equivalents.”
  • “I can convert when I have a model or prompt.”
  • “I can convert and explain why the values are equal.”

Helpful self-talk

“The number is not changing. I am only changing how it is written.”

Reflection

  • A common equivalent I can now recall is...
  • A visual model that helped me was...
  • A conversion step I want to practise again is...
🛠 Final Task

Assignment: Design a Fair and Clear Market Offer

Create a market offer that communicates the same discount in at least three equivalent forms. Present it as a poster, digital advert, report or museum-style display.

Your final product must include:

  • a fraction, decimal and percentage that are equivalent
  • a visual model such as a hundred square, bar or number line
  • an original price and correctly calculated sale price
  • an explanation of how you know the representations are equivalent
  • a judgement about which representation is clearest and why
  • a reflection on how you checked your calculations

KS3 readiness

This task prepares learners to move fluently between representations, compare proportions, calculate percentages and justify mathematical decisions.

Final Task Rubric

Criteria Emerging Developing Secure Extending
Conceptual Understanding I can recognise some familiar fractions, decimals or percentages with support. I can match familiar equivalent forms using a visual model or prompt. I can explain why equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages represent the same quantity. I can generalise conversion methods and explain equivalence using more than one representation.
Fluency & Accuracy I can complete some conversions with support. I can convert familiar values, though I may make occasional calculation or notation errors. I can accurately convert, simplify, compare and order fractions, decimals and percentages. I can work efficiently with less familiar values and check using alternative methods.
Application to Problems I can attempt a familiar problem when the representation and method are given. I can use equivalence in familiar price, measure or sharing problems with some support. I can apply equivalence to solve practical problems and explain whether my answer is reasonable. I can design, compare and evaluate representations for different audiences and purposes.
Independence & Reflection I can begin with support and identify when I need help. I can use a suggested model or conversion strategy and respond to feedback. I can choose a suitable strategy, check my work and explain what helped me learn. I can work independently, refine my design and reflect on how representation affects understanding.

Exit question: If the amount stays the same, why might one representation be more useful than another?
