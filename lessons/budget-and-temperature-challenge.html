🧠 The Knowing World

Number sense is more than finding an exact answer

Strong number sense includes predicting the likely size and sign of an answer, selecting a suitable method and checking whether the result is reasonable.

The Estimate–Calculate–Check cycle

  1. Estimate: predict an approximate result or sensible range.
  2. Calculate: use an efficient mental, written or digital method.
  3. Check: use an inverse operation, alternative method or comparison with the estimate.

£1,248.75 − £786.40
Estimate: £1,250 − £790 ≈ £460
Exact answer: £462.35
Check: £786.40 + £462.35 = £1,248.75

Temperature change

A temperature rise can be represented by a positive integer. A fall can be represented by a negative integer.

Start at −5°C.
Rise by 7°C: −5 + 7 = 2°C.
Fall by 4°C: 2 − 4 = −2°C.

Crossing zero

When a change crosses zero, zero can be used as a checkpoint. The change from −6°C to 4°C is 10°C: six degrees to reach zero and four more to reach 4°C.

🌱 The Living Body

Make one decision at a time

Multi-step problems can feel crowded. Break the situation into smaller decisions and keep track of units.

  1. Underline the starting values.
  2. Identify each positive or negative change.
  3. Estimate the likely outcome.
  4. Complete one calculation at a time.
  5. Check whether the sign, unit and size make sense.
  6. Explain the decision in your own words.

Confidence check

  • “I can estimate a budget total before calculating.”
  • “I can track a sequence of temperature changes.”
  • “I can calculate a remaining balance.”
  • “I can justify a decision using mathematical evidence.”

Helpful self-talk

“I do not need to solve everything at once. I can estimate, calculate, check and then decide.”

🛠 Final Task

Assignment: Create a Community Winter Support Proposal

Create a report, presentation, dashboard, poster or broadcast that recommends how a fictional community centre should use a limited budget during a period of cold weather.

Your final product must include:

  • a starting budget
  • at least four planned expenses
  • an estimated total cost
  • an exact total cost
  • a remaining-balance calculation
  • an inverse or alternative check
  • at least four signed temperature readings
  • a temperature sequence containing a rise and a fall
  • one change that crosses zero
  • a temperature alert threshold
  • a budget reserve threshold
  • a justified recommendation linked to fairness or safety
  • a reflection identifying the most important mathematical evidence

KS3 readiness

This task brings together place value, decimal calculation, estimation, negative integers, multi-step reasoning, checking and mathematical communication.
